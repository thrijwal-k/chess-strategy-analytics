# Openings explained

## What an opening is
An opening is the sequence of first moves of a game. Common openings have names, such as the London System or the
Sicilian Defence. Openings matter because they decide what kind of position arises, but players of different strength
choose different openings, so comparing raw win rates of openings is misleading. This project compares each opening
with what the players' ratings predict.

## White system and Black defence
White chooses a system (how White sets up) and Black chooses a defence (how Black replies). A matchup is always a
White system against a Black defence. Two White openings cannot meet each other: the Queen's Gambit and the King's
Gambit are both played by White, so "Queen's Gambit against King's Gambit" cannot happen on the board. In the same way
the London System (which starts with 1.d4) can never meet the Caro-Kann (which answers 1.e4).

## Named openings and ECO codes
The Lichess opening catalogue lists 3,865 named lines grouped into 150 families, each with an ECO code (A00 to E99)
from the Encyclopaedia of Chess Openings. This project labels every game with the deepest catalogue position it
reaches, matching positions rather than move orders so that the same position always gets the same name.

## London System
The London System is a White system: 1.d4, then the dark-squared bishop to f4 early, usually followed by e3, Nf3, c3
and Bd3. It is popular with club players because White can use the same setup against almost any defence. The Jobava
London is a sharper version with Nc3 instead of c3. In this project the London's edge over the rating prediction is
largest for lower-rated players.

## Dutch Defence
The Dutch Defence is a Black defence to 1.d4 that starts 1...f5, fighting for the e4 square. Its main setups are the
Leningrad (with ...g6 and ...Bg7), the Stonewall (pawns on d5, e6 and f5) and the Classical (with ...e6 and ...d6).

## Queen's Gambit
The Queen's Gambit is a White opening: 1.d4 d5 2.c4. White offers the c-pawn to gain control of the centre. Black can
take it (Queen's Gambit Accepted, 2...dxc4) or decline it (Queen's Gambit Declined, usually 2...e6, or the Slav with
2...c6). The Netflix series The Queen's Gambit (October 2020) is named after it.

## King's Gambit
The King's Gambit is a White opening: 1.e4 e5 2.f4. White offers the f-pawn for quick development and attack. It was
popular in the 1800s and is rarer today.

## Sicilian Defence
The Sicilian Defence is Black's most popular answer to 1.e4: 1.e4 c5. It leads to unbalanced positions. White's main
choices against it include the Open Sicilian (an early d4), the Alapin (2.c3), the Closed Sicilian, the Grand Prix
Attack and the Smith-Morra Gambit.

## Caro-Kann Defence
The Caro-Kann Defence is a solid Black defence to 1.e4: 1.e4 c6 followed by 2...d5. In this project it is one of the
best defences for Black relative to the ratings, and its share of all games rose from about 3% to over 5% between
2016 and 2026.

## French Defence
The French Defence is a Black defence to 1.e4: 1.e4 e6 followed by 2...d5. It is solid but can leave Black's
light-squared bishop blocked in.

## Italian Game, Ruy Lopez and Scotch Game
These are White openings after 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6. The Italian Game continues 3.Bc4, the Ruy Lopez (or Spanish) 3.Bb5
and the Scotch Game 3.d4. They are among the oldest and most studied openings.

## King's Indian Defence and Grünfeld
Both are Black defences to 1.d4 in which Black fianchettoes the dark-squared bishop with ...g6 and ...Bg7. In the
King's Indian Black lets White build a big centre and attacks it later; in the Grünfeld Black strikes at it at once
with ...d5. In this project the King's Indian is the London System's hardest opponent.

## Gambits
A gambit is an opening where one side sacrifices material, usually a pawn, on purpose. Players sacrifice pawns to
gain faster development, open lines towards the enemy king or take the initiative, betting that the activity is worth
more than the pawn. Examples
are the Queen's Gambit, King's Gambit, Smith-Morra Gambit, Benko Gambit and Englund Gambit.

## Fianchetto
A fianchetto is developing a bishop to the long diagonal by moving the knight's pawn one square (g3 or b3 for White,
...g6 or ...b6 for Black) and putting the bishop on g2, b2, g7 or b7.
